## evofx

### Deviations
- The contract asks for Body.PetFx to be a Folder of Attachments. I made it a single Attachment instead. Roblox only places an Attachment in the world when its parent is a BasePart, so an Attachment inside a Folder never emits anything. The attachment keeps the name PetFx under Body and holds everything: FxMain (the element ParticleEmitter), FxAura (stage 2 only, the aura glints in the pet's own Glow / GlowB colours) and FxLight (the PointLight). Its attributes are Stage, Element, Detail, Rate, Unit and Pulse. PetFx.Clear removes it.
- Particle sizes and speeds follow the pet's own bounding box: one unit U is the pet's largest side divided by 7. The attachment sits at the box centre, not at the Body root, because the root sits low near the belly. This way one recipe fits small first evolutions, 8-stud second evolutions, opts.Scale and the future mesh pets. The PointLight numbers are exactly the contract's (0.6 / range 6 and 1.0 / range 8), multiplied by max(1, Scale) and capped at 60.
- The rate budget (12 on High, 4 on Low) counts only PetFx's own emitters. The older RaritySparkles (3-5/s) and FinishSparkles (3/s) are separate, so a Legendary Golden pet at stage 2 on High can reach about 20/s in total.
- Stage 2 Neon pulse: PetFx sets PB_Pulse on the Neon parts whose name starts with Aura, at most 32 on High and 12 on Low to keep Animate cheap. It also flags them with the attribute PFX_Pulse so Clear can undo it.
- Low graphics quality on the client: PetFx.SetQuality('Low'|'High') turns off every emitter tagged 'PetFx' (CollectionService). That includes emitters already in the game, pets built later, and server-built pets that stream in later (through the tag-added signal). The light stays on. Switching back to High turns on exactly the emitters PetFx turned off. Until something calls SetQuality, the client follows Roblox's own graphics slider (1-3 counts as Low). After that it hooks itself to GraphicsQuality + ClientSettings.Changed, found under PlayerScripts.Client with a deferred lookup that never yields inside Build.

### Notes for next waves

API: PetFx.Apply(model, look, stage, opts) returns Body.PetFx (an Attachment) or nil. opts: Detail, Scale, NoFx, Viewport (+ Fx = true to force effects in a viewport), Element (override). Apply calls Clear first, so it is safe to call again (idempotent); a Clone keeps one PetFx. Other functions: PetFx.Clear(model); PetFx.Spec(element, stage, detail) gives pure numbers (Main / Aura / Light / Rate / Wide / Grow); PetFx.ElementOf(look[, override]) picks the element in this order: look.Element, catalog Base/Id (also '@Golden' copies), the hybrid's StyleId, the species default, then Celestial; PetFx.SetQuality / PetFx.Quality; PetFx.TAG = 'PetFx'; PetFx.Elements (the 8 recipes). The aura colours come from PetBuilder.EvolutionColors(def, 2), required lazily (PetBuilder requires PetFx at load, so there is no circular-require problem). For mesh pets it falls back to Neon parts named Glow / GlowB (or MK_Key) and the Aura group. Mesh path (runtime-pets W2): give MeshLibrary.Assemble an invisible `Body` BasePart root and call PetFx.Apply after assembly. Everything adapts to the model's GetBoundingBox. No Parts, no textures, no per-frame Lua. Look per element (default sparkle sprite, softened with LightEmission <= 0.7 and Brightness 1.1 so colours read on the bright P4 sky): Flame = gold-to-red embers that pop out and float up; Frost = white / ice-blue snowflakes puffed out that drift down, turning slowly; Storm = short blue-violet spark streaks (VelocityParallel + Squash); Nature = tumbling green leaves blown sideways (LightEmission 0, lit by the scene); Water = aqua bubbles that rise, swell and pop; Earth = amber-to-mint gem glints thrown up that twinkle and fall back; Celestial = twinkling white-gold star motes (the owl looked the most magical); Shadow = soft violet-to-magenta wisps stretching upward. Stage 2 adds a slowly turning shell of glints in the pet's Glow colours, locked to the pet, plus a brighter, bigger light and pulsing aura bits. Stage 1 is a gentle trickle of about 6/s; Low detail is sparse (at most 4/s). Five preview rounds: the first was invisible (particles too small and spawned at the low root), the second washed out to white (additive blending + Brightness on a bright sky), then I tuned saturation, sizes and the spark lifetime. The previews are a numpy approximation of Roblox particles, so the final colour / rate check should happen in Studio Play. Gotcha: the default ParticleEmitter Texture is the sparkle sprite in Roblox but '' in the mock; tests check Texture == '' (never set). The checkpoint commit 3e5ef9e already contains an earlier copy of my files; the working tree has the final versions (PetFx.lua and dump_model.lua are modified).

## kit

### Deviations
- Low MeshPart budget: the recipe asks for a Low set (body + wings + accessory) of 14 or fewer parts. Keeping the 4.3 name contract (one part per palette key, and keys referenced by name are never baked) gives 29-31 parts, so catalog setLow.parts is 32. Every other recipe 4.2 limit holds: High sets have at most 6.5k tris and 40 parts, Low sets at most 3k tris. tools/mesh_budget.py also shows 1467 MeshParts in the 16-player lobby against the 400 draw-call budget. Roblox instances identical meshes, so actual draw calls follow distinct keys, not copies, but this needs measuring in Studio.
- The 'every part closed' check runs per GROUP (net face area of 0 over each group), not per part. Inside a group the parts are open where one key meets another, because faces between opaque keys are culled. Closing each part separately would roughly double the hidden triangles.
- Only the FBX CreationTimeStamp is zeroed. Blender already writes a constant FileId and CreationTime. The remaining non-determinism came from object UIDs built from Python string hashes, so build_all re-runs itself with PYTHONHASHSEED=0. Two full runs gave byte-identical FBX files and meshes.json.
- Colour recipes are fitted from the bridge (40+ probe Looks per pet, then the colors.py ops plus the 'shadeLight' and 'headroom' ops), not hand-written. All 50-52 palette keys of dog, cat and dragon fit exactly.
- Mouth, blush and teeth are re-drawn as design cells at the mesh K. At finer K the Lua dot() painting left gaps in these strokes.
- The cat and dragon High bodies stepped from K 1.38 to 1.242 by the budget rule (per-key pet budget 4750 so a body plus Feather wings plus Crown fits 6.5k). The cat Low body stepped from 0.86 to 0.774.
- Added a 'calibration' catalog class (cal_axes plus the test key prop_portal_reference with upload:false, which writes to blender/out only).
